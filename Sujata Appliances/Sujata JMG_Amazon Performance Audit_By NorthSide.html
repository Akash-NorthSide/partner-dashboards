Ad Spend Efficiency — Strategic Investment

ⓘ How to Read This Tab
Indexed Glance Views (iGV)
The number of times a product detail page was viewed. Think of it as "eyeballs on listing." More GVs = more traffic, but not necessarily more sales.
Conv % (Conversion Rate)
Percentage of glance views that resulted in a sale. Higher is better. 1.20% average means ~1 sale per 83 page views.
Rev/GV (Revenue per Glance View)
How much revenue each page view generates on average. Higher Rev/GV = more valuable traffic. ₹65 average across the portfolio.
Gap (pp = percentage points)
The absolute difference between GV Share % and Sales Share %. Measured in percentage points (pp). Example: Delhi has 5.6% GV share and 4.1% sales share → gap is +1.5pp. Positive gap = high traffic, low conversion (optimize listings). Negative gap = strong converter, grow traffic.
Daily Trend %
Month-over-month change in daily GV volume, normalized per day (Feb = 24 days, Mar = 26 days). This ensures a fair comparison despite unequal month lengths. Positive = growing traffic, negative = declining.
Spend/GV
How much ad spend is invested per glance view. Lower is more efficient. ₹16.90/GV (Electric Kettle) vs ₹2.49/GV (Mixer Blender) shows wide variation in traffic acquisition cost.
Zero-Sale GV Share
Percentage of total glance views coming from ASINs that generated zero sales. At 10.3%, this represents wasted traffic — listings that attract visitors but fail to convert.

Comprehensive Glossary

Ad-ROAS (Return on Ad Spend)
Ad Sales ÷ Ad Spend. 24.2x means ₹24.20 ad sales per ₹1 ad spend. Benchmark: 3x+ is profitable, 5x+ is strong, 10x+ is excellent.
bROAS (Blended ROAS)
Total Retail Sales ÷ Ad Spend. 59.0x means ₹59 total revenue per ₹1 ad spend. Includes both ad-driven and organic sales.
ACOS (Advertising Cost of Sales)
Ad spend ÷ attributed sales × 100%. Inverse of Ad-ROAS. If ACOS = 5%, you spend ₹5 to make ₹100 in ads revenue. 3-8% is typical.
Ad Dependency (Ad Dependency Ratio)
Ad-attributed sales ÷ Pi Net sales × 100%. Measures what % of total sales require ads to happen. 40% = 40% of sales need ads; 60% sell organically. Higher = more risky.
Ad-Attributed Sales
Sales Amazon credits to your ads. Includes 7-14 day post-click attribution. Not all conversions are attributed even if ads helped.
Pi Net Sales
Total sales (attributed + organic). Complete view of product performance. Includes all channels, all touchpoints.
Retail Sales
Total sales from all channels (Pi Net Sales). Includes organic and ad-attributed.
Ad Sales
Sales attributed to ad clicks only (AMS data). Subset of Retail Sales.
Ad-Units
Units sold through ad-attributed orders (AMS data).
Net-Units
Total units sold across all channels — organic + ad-driven (Pi Sales data).
CTR (Click-Through Rate)
Clicks ÷ impressions × 100%. Measures ad appeal. 0.5-1% is typical; 2%+ is excellent.
CPC (Cost Per Click)
Ad spend ÷ clicks. How much you pay per click. Varies by competition, relevance, and match type.
CPA (Cost Per Acquisition)
Ad spend ÷ conversions (purchases). ₹500 CPA = you spend ₹500 per sale. Lower is better.
ASIN (Amazon Standard Identification Number)
10-character unique product ID. Each size, color, or variant = separate ASIN. 47 ASINs = 47 product variants.
SP (Sponsored Products)
Your main ad type. Ads appear in search results & on detail pages. Includes Auto, Keyword, ASIN, Category targeting.
SB (Sponsored Brands)
Header-style ads showcasing your brand logo + multiple products. Drives brand awareness + conversion. Typically higher Ad-ROAS than SP.
SD (Sponsored Display)
Retargeting & audience ads. Reaches people across Amazon & partner sites. Lower Ad-ROAS but important for brand reach.
Auto Targeting
Amazon's algorithm matches your product to searches & products. Low setup but requires monitoring for low-converting matches.
Keyword Targeting
You define keywords. Better control, higher relevance. Takes more setup & optimization.
ASIN Targeting (Product Targeting)
Ads appear when viewing competitor products or similar items. Highest intent. Smallest reach but best conversion.
Category Targeting
Ads appear when browsing specific categories. Broader reach than ASIN, lower intent.
Branded Keywords
Your brand name + variants ("Sujata", "Sujata mixer"). Your audience. Highest Ad-ROAS, lowest competition.
Generic Keywords
Product type ("mixer grinder", "juicer"). Broader reach, moderate intent. Good volume, medium Ad-ROAS.
Competitor Keywords
Rival brand names ("Havells mixer", "Inalsa juicer"). Capture switchers. Medium intent, good Ad-ROAS.
Long-Tail Keywords
Longer, specific phrases ("best mixer grinder under 3000", "wet grinder for sambar"). Lower volume, high intent.
Tier 1 Products
Your stars. High revenue, high Ad-ROAS, consistent performance. Examples: Mixer Grinder, JMG.
Tier 2 Products
Solid performers. Decent revenue, good Ad-ROAS, growth potential. Examples: Personal Blender, Mixer Blender.
Tier 3 Products
Problem products. Low Ad-ROAS, high ad dependency. Either restructure or pause. Examples: Electric Kettle, Citrus Juicer.
Attribution Window
Time period Amazon credits a sale to an ad. Amazon uses 7-14 day window. Sales outside this window aren't attributed.
Ad Spend Budget
Daily or monthly limit you set. Amazon tries to reach it by adjusting bids, not guaranteed if no qualified traffic.
Bid Strategy
How aggressively you bid. Options: Dynamic (up & down), Down only, Fixed. Affects placement & spend.
Negative Keywords
Terms you don't want. Example: add "price" as negative if you're a premium brand. Prevents wasted spend on irrelevant searches.
PPC (Pay-Per-Click)
You pay only when someone clicks your ad. Amazon ads are PPC.
Impression
Your ad was shown once. Not a click, not a sale. Thousands of impressions needed to get a few clicks.
Conversion Rate
Clicks that result in sales ÷ total clicks × 100%. Amazon average is 1-3%. Higher = better product/ad match.
Search Volume
# of searches for a keyword. More searches = more potential clicks. Brand Analytics provides estimates.
Seasonality
Sales patterns varying by season (holidays, festivals, weather). Plan budgets accounting for high/low seasons.

FAQ & Common Pitfalls

Question Answer
Why doesn't attributed sales = total sales? Amazon uses 7-14 day attribution. Sales outside this window (e.g., someone clicked 20 days ago but bought today) aren't credited. Also, organic sales (no click) aren't attributed to ads.
Is 24.2x Ad-ROAS good? Yes, excellent. Industry benchmark is 3-5x. You're at 24.2x Ad-ROAS (59.0x bROAS), top 5%. But Tier 3 is pulling you down; fix that to reach 35x Ad-ROAS.
Should I pause all Tier 3 products? No. Pause worst performers (Electric Kettle 1.9x, Citrus Juicer 3.0x). Rebuild others with keyword + ASIN targeting. Tier 3 can work at 8-12x with restructuring.
Why are keyword campaigns 39.8x Ad-ROAS while Auto is only 16.1x Ad-ROAS? Keywords = you chose high-intent terms. Auto = Amazon's broad matching. Keywords require more work but deliver better ROI. Increase keyword budget share.
How do I know if I'm overspending on ads? If Ad-ROAS < 3x, you're losing money on ads alone. If Ad Dependency > 70%, too risky. Your overall Ad-ROAS is 24.2x, so not overspending, but Tier 3 is.
Should I increase budget? Not yet. First, optimize Tier 3 & expand to 200+ competitors + 40% BA keywords. Once projected improvements are proven, then scale spend.
Why East zone is underperforming? 8.4% vs 15% target. Possible reasons: (a) lower income penetration, (b) language/localization gap, (c) insufficient budget allocation. Test localized campaigns.
What's the difference between SB and SP? SP (Sponsored Products) = search ads, main revenue driver. SB (Sponsored Brands) = header ads, brand builder. Both PPC. SB typically has higher Ad-ROAS but lower volume.
